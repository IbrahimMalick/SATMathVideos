[[beat:beauty_word]] The Saviour of Mankind begins in an exceptionally
beautiful desert in Arabia. I want you to remember one first word: beauty.
Beautiful land, beautiful sky, beautiful language. [[beat:beauty_inside]]
But as we are about to discover, beauty on the outside does not necessarily
mean everything is right on the inside. Think about it for a minute, because
this saviour is a very interesting story. What is the first scene of it? It
is beauty, isn't it? [[beat:unparalleled]] Just like we imagined Arabia,
let's imagine it again. The book calls it a land of unparalleled charm and

I say that you are a good speaker. If I say that your eloquence is
unparalleled, that means there is no comparison. [[beat:unparalleled_sense]]
So the beauty that the author is describing is unparalleled charm and
beauty. Think about that. A beauty that has no comparison. The writer is not
saying that it's a nice city, it's an okay city. It possessed a beauty
difficult to match, and Makkah was situated in this environment. Remember,

[[beat:surprising]] But now the chapter does something interesting. It
doesn't immediately tell us about religion. It tells us something surprising
about the people. [[beat:memory]] The Arabs, according to this chapter,
possessed a remarkable memory. Their memory was very strong. Think about it.
They didn't have Google. They didn't have a phone. They didn't go and ask
ChatGPT. But imagine a culture where memory itself is one of your most
important technologies. [[beat:ukaz]] Language was tremendously important,
public speaking was important. And the text tells us that every year there
was a fair at Ukaz, where there was a poetry competition. And a poet used to
recite a poem of hundreds of lines from his memory.
[[beat:eloquence]] Now you should learn one more word here. That word is
eloquence. Eloquence does not simply mean talking a lot. There are many
people who talk for hours. But the meaning of eloquence is the ability to
express thoughts beautifully, clearly and powerfully.
[[beat:brilliance_word]] So picture these people. They have a great memory,
they have very good speech, their poetry is extraordinary, their language is
very rich. So our second word of this chapter is brilliance.

[[beat:contradiction]] And now we reach the great contradiction at the heart
of the opening. How can you have beauty and brilliance and still have a
serious problem? Think about it. There is beauty, there is brilliance, but
there is also a serious problem. [[beat:wisdom]] The reason for this is that
intelligence and eloquence alone do not guarantee wisdom. A person can be
very clever, but despite that he can make many mistakes. A society can be
very culturally impressive, but can also be morally lost. And that takes us
to our third word. [[beat:breakdown_word]] And that third word in the
chapter is breakdown. The textbook describes civilization in the fifth and
sixth centuries as standing on the verge of chaos.
[[beat:verge]] Now what does the word verge mean? The word verge means the
edge of something. Imagine that you are standing on a cliff where if you
take one more step forward, you will fall. So on the verge of chaos means
that you are very close to destruction and disorder. Chaos means complete
confusion and disorder. [[beat:crumbling]] This chapter is giving us another
powerful image. Civilization, which took thousands of years to build, is
suddenly crumbling. Like you have built a beautiful building, and first
there was a crack, then another crack, then a block fell from somewhere,
then the roof began to fall, and eventually the entire building fell. That
is the picture the writer creates. [[beat:contrast_sentence]] So memorize
this contrast. Listen carefully. The Arabs possessed brilliant language, but
humanity faced a moral crisis. That's a much stronger understanding of the
chapter than saying that people were bad before Islam.
[[beat:sophisticated]] Because the writer is making a very sophisticated
point. There was ability, there was civilization, there was culture, but
something essential was missing. What was missing? Direction, purpose, moral
guidance. [[beat:three_words]] So our first three words are beauty,
brilliance, breakdown. Now everything changes.

[[beat:cave_scene]] Now what is this word? The cave. Now imagine Makkah.
People are walking in their lives, markets are open, trade is happening,
arguments are happening, people are telling each other long poems, business
is happening, there is noise. But far away from all the noise, our Prophet,
peace be upon him, is sitting in a cave, in very deep thought.
[[beat:solitude]] What does the textbook call it? Solitude and meditation,
in the cave of Hira. Now, the meaning of solitude is not being alone. It is
not loneliness. You choose solitude. You decide by yourself that you should
sit away from others, away from noise, and think. You deliberately move away
from people and noise. [[beat:meditation]] And meditation is deep
reflection, deep thinking. [[beat:inside_outside]] Now picture the contrast.
Outside the cave there is noise; inside the cave there is silence. Outside
the cave the whole society is rushing around; inside the cave there is
reflection. Outside the cave there is a world of problems. In the cave, our
Prophet was thinking about those problems. [[beat:cave_word]] This is our
fourth word, which you have to remember: cave. And I want you to remember
something important. Great change often begins quietly. The big changes are
made in silence. It doesn't have to be on the battlefield. It doesn't have
to be in a palace. It doesn't have to be in front of a big crowd. Sometimes
history begins in silence, in a cave.

[[beat:compassion]] Now look at scene five: compassion. What the chapter
says is that he possessed profound compassion for humanity and was troubled
by wrong beliefs and social evils. So compassion is more than feeling sorry
for someone. It means recognizing another person's suffering and genuinely
caring about it. [[beat:sympathy]] If you look at someone going through a
hardship and just feel sorry for them, that is not compassion. If you see a
small child begging, you can say that's unfortunate. This is sympathy, but
it is not compassion, because compassion takes you towards action. So the
chapter presents this not merely as intellectual discussion; it presents
deep concern for people. [[beat:revelation]] And then comes the turning
point. One day, in the cave of Hira, Hazrat Jibril came. The chapter states
that the first revelation was conveyed there, beginning with the command:
Read. Iqra. [[beat:calling_word]] Now what is our fifth memory word, which
we have to remember? Calling. The story has moved from beauty to brilliance
to breakdown to cave to calling. And from this point onward the quiet
reflection becomes a public mission.

[[beat:message]] The message. The revelation continued over the following
twenty-three years, and the central message: the oneness of Allah, the unity
of mankind. The chapter says his mission sought to destroy what it describes
as a nexus of superstition, ignorance and disbelief, and lead humanity
toward faith and divine bliss. [[beat:direction_change]] Now notice how the
chapter has changed direction. In the beginning society is close to chaos;
then a message of direction appears. In the beginning, confusion; now,
clarity. At the beginning, moral darkness; now, guidance. This contrast is
central to the chapter.

[[beat:resistance]] Good. Scene seven: resistance. Here is something
students often forget. Great change usually creates resistance, especially
when change challenges people's beliefs, habits, status or power. This
chapter tells us that the new message threatened the existing society.
Pressure began mounting on Rasoolullah, peace be upon him, and his
followers. [[beat:renounce]] The people opposing him attempted to make him
renounce his cause. They tried very hard. Renounce means to give something
up publicly or formally. In other words: stop, give up, walk away, return to
the old way. [[beat:sun_moon]] And now comes one of the most memorable
moments in the chapter. Rasoolullah responds to his uncle with extraordinary
determination. Even if the sun were placed in one hand and the moon in the
other, he would not abandon the proclamation of the oneness of Allah. The
textbook then describes his uncle as being deeply impressed.
[[beat:courage_word]] This is the sixth memory word: courage. Not courage
when everything is easy. When everything is going well, then everyone
becomes brave and says, I have a lot of courage. Courage when stopping would
have been easier. Courage when pressure increases. Courage when people
oppose you. [[beat:conviction]] Remember: conviction is easy to claim when
it costs nothing. The chapter shows conviction when there is a price
attached to it.

[[beat:transformation]] Now we reach the final movement. The chapter says
Rasoolullah continued the mission despite challenges. It describes his
message and leadership as producing enormous spiritual and worldly change.
It even cites Michael Hart, author of The 100: A Ranking of the Most
Influential Persons in History, commenting on the extraordinary religious
and secular influence of Rasoolullah. [[beat:change_word]] Then comes
another powerful word: transformation. Not improvement, not adjustment.
Transformation: a major or complete change. Think, a caterpillar becomes a
butterfly. That's transformation. The chapter's argument is that the mission
brought transformation in beliefs, values and society.

[[beat:whole_story]] Now look at our entire story. Beauty, a remarkable
land. Brilliance, a remarkable people with memory and eloquence. Breakdown,

disconnected facts, only one story.

[[beat:title]] Now look at this. What is the title? The Saviour of Mankind.

[[beat:grammar_noun]] The textbook expects you to understand parts of
speech. So today I only want you to remember three. Noun. What is a noun? A
noun names something. Person: Muhammad, peace be upon him. Place: Makkah.
Thing: cave. Idea: compassion. Person, place, thing, idea, these are all
nouns. [[beat:grammar_verb]] Now what is a verb? A verb tells us what
happens or what something does. That is action. Examples are lived,
possessed, recited, proclaimed, changed. These are all verbs.
[[beat:grammar_conj]] Then what is a conjunction? A conjunction joins ideas.
What is there in this example? And, but, because. Now listen to this. The
Arabs were eloquent, but civilization was facing a crisis. Arabs and
civilization are nouns. Were and was facing function as verbs. And but is
the bridge connecting two contrasting ideas. [[beat:but_idea]] And that word
but is almost the entire first half of this chapter. The Arabs were
talented, but the society was troubled. One little conjunction can carry a
huge idea. That makes grammar easier to remember.

[[beat:main_idea]] One more skill you can be asked is the main idea. That's
the one final English skill. Every paragraph contains many words, but not
every word is equally important. Ask yourself: if I could remember only one

else that explains, proves or illustrates it is the supporting detail.
[[beat:drama]] Just like we see in dramas, there is a main character in it,

details. Find the sentence hiding underneath all the sentences.
