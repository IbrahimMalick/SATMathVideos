---
scene: concept.pronoun_agreement
---

[[beat:hook]] A single word can quietly confuse an entire paragraph. The
sentence may begin with one person. Then, without warning, the pronoun
starts talking about several people. Or the writer begins with we and
suddenly switches to you. Most listeners may not immediately know what is
wrong. But they can feel that something does not match. [[beat:promise]] By
the end of this lesson, you will know how to catch these hidden pronoun
mistakes. You will learn how to connect every pronoun to the correct noun.
You will also discover why words such as team, company, and committee can be
surprisingly tricky. [[beat:challenge]] And near the end, I will give you a
sentence with a distorted reflection. See whether you can repair it before I
reveal the answer. [[beat:mirror]] Let us begin with a mirror. Imagine that
a giant stands in front of a mirror. What should appear in the reflection? A
giant. The mirror should not suddenly show a tiny kitten. That would not be
a reflection. It would be a completely different creature.
[[beat:pronoun_def]] Pronouns work in the same way. A pronoun is a word that
replaces a noun. Words such as he, she, it, they, we, and you are pronouns.
The noun that a pronoun refers to is called its antecedent. Antecedent
simply means the word that came before and gave the pronoun its identity.
[[beat:cacao]] For example: The cacao bean develops its flavor during
processing. The noun is bean. Bean is singular. Therefore, its pronoun must
also be singular. We use its. [[beat:cacao_plural]] Now listen to the plural
version. The cacao beans develop their flavor during processing. The noun is
beans. Beans is plural. Therefore, the pronoun must also be plural. We use
their. [[beat:mirror_rule]] The cacao bean develops its flavor. The cacao
beans develop their flavor. The noun stands in front of the mirror. The
pronoun is its reflection. They must match. This is called pronoun
agreement. A singular noun needs a singular pronoun. A plural noun needs a
plural pronoun.

[[beat:bicycle]] Consider this sentence: The bicycle lost their front wheel.
What is the noun? Bicycle. How many bicycles are there? One. But the pronoun
their is plural. The reflection does not match. [[beat:bicycle_fix]] The
correct sentence is: The bicycle lost its front wheel. One bicycle. Its
wheel. [[beat:students]] Now consider this sentence: The students submitted
her assignments before noon. The noun students is plural. But her refers to
one female person. The sentence needs a plural pronoun. The students
submitted their assignments before noon. Students and their now match.
[[beat:method1]] Here is the first method to remember. Whenever you see a
pronoun, look backward. Find the noun that the pronoun is replacing. Then
ask: Is that noun singular or plural? The answer will usually tell you which
pronoun you need.

[[beat:collective]] However, some nouns can be deceptive. Consider words
such as team, company, committee, family, jury, and audience. These words
describe groups of people. Because several people are involved, you may be
tempted to use a plural pronoun. But in formal American English, these
collective nouns are usually treated as singular when the group acts as one
unit. [[beat:theater]] For example: The theater company announced its new
season. The company includes many people. There may be actors, directors,
designers, and stage managers. But the company is being discussed as one
organization. Therefore, we use its. The theater company announced its new
season. Not: The theater company announced their new season.
[[beat:team_ex]] Here is another example: The team changed its strategy
during the second half. The team contains several players. But the sentence
treats the team as one unit. So the pronoun is its.
[[beat:more_collective]] The committee published its final report. The jury
reached its decision. The organization updated its policy. Each collective
noun represents one group acting as a single unit. Therefore, each one
receives a singular reflection.

[[beat:camera]] But pronoun agreement is not only about singular and plural
nouns. It is also about keeping the same point of view. Imagine that a
camera is recording a scene from one position. Halfway through the scene,
the camera suddenly jumps into someone else's hands. The audience becomes
confused. Pronouns can create the same problem. [[beat:we_you]] Consider
this sentence: When we prepare for an important presentation, you should
practice several times. The sentence begins with we. Then it suddenly
switches to you. Who is the sentence talking about? Us? Or the listener? The
perspective has changed. [[beat:we_fix]] To keep the sentence consistent, we
could say: When we prepare for an important presentation, we should practice
several times. Now the pronouns match. We prepare. We should practice. We
could also write: When you prepare for an important presentation, you should
practice several times. You prepare. You should practice. Either version can
work. The problem is not choosing we or you. The problem is switching
between them without a reason. [[beat:person]] Listen to another example: A
person should carefully review your work before submitting it. The sentence
begins with a person. Then it switches to your. The mirror has changed. One
possible correction is: You should carefully review your work before
submitting it. Another possible correction is: A person should carefully
review their work before submitting it. The exact correction depends on the
intended meaning. [[beat:pov_rule]] But the important principle remains the
same. Keep the point of view consistent. If a paragraph begins with we,
continue with we, us, and our. If it begins with you, continue with you and
your. If it begins with they, continue with they, them, and their. Do not
allow the pronouns to change identities in the middle of the paragraph.

[[beat:maya]] There is one more problem to watch for. Sometimes, a pronoun
could refer to more than one noun. Consider this sentence: When Maya spoke
to Elena, she seemed nervous. Who seemed nervous? Maya? Or Elena? The
pronoun she has two possible antecedents. The reflection is unclear because
two people are standing in front of the mirror. [[beat:maya_fix]] To repair
the sentence, replace the pronoun with the intended name. When Maya spoke to
Elena, Maya seemed nervous. Or: When Maya spoke to Elena, Elena seemed
nervous. A pronoun should not force the reader to guess. Its antecedent
should be clear.

[[beat:distorted]] Now it is time for the distorted reflection challenge.
Listen carefully. The research team completed their investigation and
published its findings. Can you hear the problem? The sentence begins with
the collective noun team. Because the team is acting as one unit, the
pronouns should remain singular. But the sentence first uses their and then
uses its. The reflection changes halfway through. [[beat:distorted_fix]] The
corrected sentence is: The research team completed its investigation and
published its findings. Team. Its investigation. Its findings. Everything
matches. [[beat:lab]] Here is another challenge. When we enter the
laboratory, you must wear protective glasses. The sentence begins with we
and changes to you. We can correct it by saying: When we enter the
laboratory, we must wear protective glasses. Or: When you enter the
laboratory, you must wear protective glasses. The camera now stays in one
position.

[[beat:strategy]] Here is the complete strategy. First, locate the pronoun.
Second, find the noun it replaces. Third, decide whether that noun is
singular or plural. Fourth, check whether the point of view remains
consistent. And finally, make sure the pronoun has only one clear
antecedent. [[beat:reflections]] Think of every pronoun as a reflection. A
singular noun needs a singular reflection. A plural noun needs a plural
reflection. A collective noun acting as one unit usually needs a singular
reflection. And a paragraph that begins with we should not suddenly
transform into you. [[beat:closing]] When the noun and pronoun match, the
mirror is clear. When they do not match, the reflection becomes distorted.
Now you know how to step in front of the mirror, examine the sentence, and
make every reflection accurate.
