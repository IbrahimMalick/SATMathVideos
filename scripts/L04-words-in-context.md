[[beat:title]] Welcome to Lecture 4. Today, you will learn how to determine
the meaning of a word based on how it is used in a passage. This is an
important skill on the Digital SAT Reading and Writing section.
[[beat:stem]] The SAT may ask: As used in the text, what does the word most
nearly mean? These questions are called words in context questions.
[[beat:goal]] The goal is not to choose the most common definition of the
word. The goal is to choose the definition that fits the sentence.
[[beat:bright]] Many English words have more than one meaning. For example,
the word bright can describe something that produces a lot of light.
[[beat:bright2]] It can also describe a person who is intelligent. The
correct meaning depends on the context.

[[beat:strategy]] The main strategy for this lecture is simple. Do not define
the word by itself. Define the word inside the sentence. Use the following
four step method. [[beat:step1]] Step one. Read the entire sentence. Do not
look only at the word being tested. The words around it provide clues.
Sometimes you may also need to read the sentence before or after it.
[[beat:step2]] Step two. Explain the sentence in your own words. Ask
yourself: What is happening in this sentence? What idea is the author
communicating? [[beat:step3]] Step three. Replace the tested word with a
simple word of your own. Do this before looking carefully at the answer
choices. [[beat:step4]] Step four. Choose the answer that is closest to your
replacement word. Then read the sentence again using your selected answer.
Make sure the meaning stays the same.

[[beat:q1_passage]] Let us look at a sample question. Although the scientist
initially doubted the new theory, the results of several experiments
compelled her to reconsider her position. [[beat:q1_stem]] As used in the
text, what does compelled most nearly mean? [[beat:q1_choices]] Answer
choice A. Forced. Answer choice B. Invited. Answer choice C. Allowed. Answer
choice D. Taught. [[beat:q1_pause]] Pause for a moment and choose your
answer. [[beat:q1_answer]] The correct answer is answer choice A. Forced.
[[beat:q1_read]] Let us solve the question step by step. First, read the
entire sentence. The scientist did not believe the theory at first. However,
the results of the experiments caused her to reconsider her opinion.
[[beat:q1_explain]] Now explain the sentence in your own words. The evidence
was so strong that the scientist had to reconsider her position.
[[beat:q1_replace]] Now replace the word compelled. We could say: The
results forced her to reconsider her position. The meaning of the sentence
remains the same. Therefore, compelled most nearly means forced.
[[beat:q1_elimB]] Now let us examine the incorrect answers. Answer choice B
is invited. The results did not politely invite the scientist to reconsider.
They strongly caused her to do so. [[beat:q1_elimC]] Answer choice C is
allowed. The results did not simply give her permission to reconsider.
[[beat:q1_elimD]] Answer choice D is taught. The experiments may have
provided information, but taught does not express the meaning of the
sentence. [[beat:q1_lesson]] The key lesson is this. A word may have several
possible meanings. Only one meaning will fit the context.

[[beat:q2_passage]] Now let us try another question. The town council
adopted a flexible approach to the construction project, changing the plan
whenever unexpected problems arose. [[beat:q2_stem]] As used in the text,
what does flexible most nearly mean? [[beat:q2_choices]] Answer choice A.
Easy to bend. Answer choice B. Willing to adapt. Answer choice C. Weakly
designed. Answer choice D. Carefully hidden. [[beat:q2_pause]] Pause for a
moment and choose your answer. [[beat:q2_answer]] The correct answer is
answer choice B. Willing to adapt. [[beat:q2_expl]] Let us examine the
sentence. The town council changed the plan when unexpected problems
appeared. This means the council was willing to adjust its approach. In this
sentence, flexible means willing to adapt. [[beat:q2_elimA]] Answer choice A
is a common meaning of flexible. A physical object may be flexible if it can
bend easily. However, the sentence is describing an approach, not a physical
object. [[beat:q2_elimC]] Answer choice C is not supported. A flexible plan
is not necessarily a weak plan. [[beat:q2_elimD]] Answer choice D is
unrelated to the sentence. [[beat:q2_trap]] This question demonstrates a
common SAT trap. The test may include the most familiar definition of the
word as an incorrect answer. Do not choose the definition you recognise
first. Choose the definition that fits the passage.

[[beat:q3_passage]] Now let us look at another example. After reviewing the
evidence, the committee determined that the witness's account was sound.
[[beat:q3_stem]] As used in the text, what does sound most nearly mean?
[[beat:q3_choices]] Answer choice A. A noise. Answer choice B. Healthy.
Answer choice C. Reliable. Answer choice D. Deep. [[beat:q3_pause]] Pause
for a moment and choose your answer. [[beat:q3_answer]] The correct answer
is answer choice C. Reliable. [[beat:q3_expl]] The committee reviewed the
evidence and evaluated the witness's account. In this context, sound means
reliable or well supported. [[beat:q3_elims]] It does not mean a noise. It
does not mean physically healthy. It does not describe depth. This is why
you must use context. The SAT often tests familiar words used in less
familiar ways.

[[beat:q4_passage]] Now let us try a more difficult question. The researcher
gave a qualified response, explaining that the early results were promising
but that more evidence was needed. [[beat:q4_stem]] As used in the text,
what does qualified most nearly mean? [[beat:q4_choices]] Answer choice A.
Fully trained. Answer choice B. Limited by conditions. Answer choice C.
Officially approved. Answer choice D. Highly intelligent. [[beat:q4_pause]]
Pause for a moment and choose your answer. [[beat:q4_answer]] The correct
answer is answer choice B. Limited by conditions. [[beat:q4_expl]] The
researcher did not fully accept the early results. The researcher said the
results were promising, but also explained that more evidence was needed.
The response contained a limitation. In this context, qualified means
cautious or limited by conditions. [[beat:q4_elims]] It does not mean
trained for a job. It does not mean officially approved. It does not mean
highly intelligent.

[[beat:contrast]] Now let us discuss another useful technique. Look for
contrast words. Words such as however, although, but, yet, and despite can
help reveal meaning. [[beat:contrast_ex1]] Consider this sentence. Although
the professor was usually reserved, she became animated when discussing her
research. [[beat:contrast_ex1b]] The word although signals a contrast. The
professor is normally reserved. But when discussing her research, she became
animated. Based on this contrast, animated probably means energetic or
excited. [[beat:contrast_ex2]] Now consider another sentence. The evidence
appeared convincing at first. However, later studies undermined the original
conclusion. [[beat:contrast_ex2b]] The word however signals a change in
direction. If later studies undermined the conclusion, they weakened it.
Therefore, undermined means weakened or made less believable. Contrast words
can give you strong clues about unfamiliar vocabulary.

[[beat:explain_tech]] Another technique is to look for explanations or
examples. [[beat:explain_ex1]] Consider this sentence. The animal is
nocturnal, meaning that it is active mainly during the night. The phrase
meaning that directly explains the word nocturnal. [[beat:explain_ex2]] Now
consider this sentence. The region contains many indigenous plants,
including species that developed naturally in the area. The example explains
that indigenous means native to a particular place. [[beat:explain_note]]
The SAT often places a definition, example, contrast, or result near the
tested word. Your job is to find that clue.

[[beat:q5_passage]] Let us try one final practice question. The architect's
original proposal was ambitious. After reviewing the limited budget,
however, she presented a more modest design. [[beat:q5_stem]] As used in the
text, what does modest most nearly mean? [[beat:q5_choices]] Answer choice
A. Shy. Answer choice B. Limited in size or cost. Answer choice C. Old
fashioned. Answer choice D. Poorly constructed. [[beat:q5_pause]] Pause for
a moment and choose your answer. [[beat:q5_answer]] The correct answer is
answer choice B. Limited in size or cost. [[beat:q5_expl]] The architect
first created an ambitious proposal. Then she reviewed the limited budget
and changed the design. The word however signals a contrast between
ambitious and modest. The budget also provides an important clue. The new
design had to be less expensive and less extensive. Therefore, modest means
limited in size or cost. [[beat:q5_elimA]] Answer choice A is a common
meaning of modest when describing a person's behaviour. However, the
sentence describes a design. [[beat:q5_elimCD]] Answer choice C is not
supported. Answer choice D is also not supported. A less expensive design is
not necessarily poorly constructed.

[[beat:traps]] Now let us review the most common traps. [[beat:trap1]] The
first trap is choosing the most common definition. The most familiar
definition may not fit the sentence. [[beat:trap2]] The second trap is
reading only the tested word. Always read the complete sentence.
[[beat:trap3]] The third trap is choosing an answer that sounds advanced. A
complicated answer is not automatically correct. [[beat:trap4]] The fourth
trap is selecting a word with the wrong tone. For example, a passage may
describe something as careful or limited. An answer that means weak or
unsuccessful may be too negative. [[beat:trap5]] The fifth trap is failing
to test the answer. Always place your answer back into the sentence. If the
meaning changes, the answer is probably wrong.

[[beat:process]] On test day, use this process. First, read the entire
sentence. Second, explain the sentence in your own words. Third, look for
clues such as contrast, examples, explanations, or results. Fourth, replace
the tested word with a simple word of your own. Fifth, choose the answer
that matches your prediction. Finally, read the sentence again with your
answer. [[beat:key]] The key idea from this lecture is simple. Context
determines meaning. Do not ask: What does this word usually mean? Ask: What
does this word mean in this sentence? [[beat:end]] That completes Lecture 4.
